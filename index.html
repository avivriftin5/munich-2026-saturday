Saturday, somewhere better than Munich

Königssee& Salzburg

A green lake in a rock canyon, a schnitzel the size of the plate, and a hangar full of Formula 1 cars. One easy day.

Out of the city

A couple of hours south, then a boat

Breakfast at the hotel, then the car from Sixt at the Hauptbahnhof at nine. The motorway runs south-east until the Alps close in, and it ends at a lake the colour of a bottle, wedged between cliffs that go straight up out of the water.

The boats are electric and almost silent — they've run on this lake since 1909, because nothing with an engine is allowed to foul it. In November there's no queue, no coach parties, and one leaves every half hour.

Halfway across the boatman cuts the motor, stands up with a trumpet and plays at the rock face to bring back the echo. It's the oldest tourist trick in Bavaria and it still works.

The far shore

St. Bartholomä

A little pilgrimage church with wine-red onion domes on a green spit of land, with the Watzmann's east face — the biggest wall in the Eastern Alps — standing 1,800 metres straight up behind it. There's no road here. Boat or nothing.

We walk the shore, look at the church, and take a boat back after about half an hour. It's 35 minutes each way on the water, and lunch in Salzburg is booked, so this isn't the place to linger. There's a hunting-lodge restaurant on the peninsula if anyone needs a coffee.

In the cold months red deer come down off the mountains to feed here. Summer visitors never see them.

Across the border

Into Salzburg

Forty-five minutes of country road north and we're in Austria — no border to speak of, no toll sticker needed the way we're going.

We park inside the Mönchsberg. The garage is cut into the mountain itself and the lift spits you out in the middle of the old town, which is a good trick for a city built between a cliff and a river.

Booked · 15:30

Lunch at Zwettler's

Kaigasse 3, two minutes from the funicular. This is the meal of the day and we're taking our time over it — there's nothing after it that can't wait.

Table booked for 15:30 — the one fixed time of the day. Park by 15:15; it's about ten minutes on foot from the garage.

Schnitzel vom Kalb — veal, the real thing, hanging off the edge of the plate with potato salad. Goulash, oxtail with polenta, roast pork, and beer they brew themselves.

Save room for one Salzburger Nockerl at the end. It's a soufflé shaped like the three hills around the city, it's meant to be shared, and it's the size of a small child. One for the table.

Dark by half four

The town, lit

By the time we walk out of lunch it's already dark, and Salzburg is one of those places that improves in the dark — the fortress floodlit above, the river running black through the middle, narrow lanes with wrought-iron guild signs hanging over them.

If anyone wants the whole city from above, the funicular up to the fortress takes two minutes and runs into the evening. If nobody does, we walk instead. Both are fine.

One last thing

Hangar-7

A dome of glass and steel by the airport, built by the man who invented Red Bull to house his aeroplanes. Inside: historic aircraft, helicopters, and the Formula 1 cars driven by Verstappen, Vettel and Räikkönen, close enough to touch — though you mustn't.

It's free to walk in, it stays open late, and it happens to sit right beside the road home. No reason not to.
