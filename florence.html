16–17 November 2026 · on my own

Florencetwo days

A hill at sunset, Gregorian chant in an empty church, Steve Hackett at nine, and the best steak in Italy the next day at half twelve.

Day one

Monday 16 November

Landing at lunchtime, and everything indoors is shut. So: the Oltrarno, the hill, and a concert.

Landing

In by one, out by half two

EN8194 from Munich touches down at 12:55. Hand luggage only, so it's straight through the terminal and into the taxi queue — Florence Airport is small enough that this is a genuinely quick business.

Ville sull'Arno sits on the Lungarno Cristoforo Colombo, a five-star villa on the river with a spa and a pool. Drop the bag, don't unpack, be back outside by 14:20.

The Uffizi, the Accademia and the Pitti are all closed on Mondays. That's not a problem — it's the reason the whole afternoon is outdoors and uphill.

The other bank

Oltrarno on foot

Taxi to Piazza Santo Spirito and start walking. Via Maggio is the antique dealers' street — gilders, framers, furniture restorers, workshops with the doors open and someone actually working inside. Then Piazza della Passera, which is about the size of a large room and one of the nicest corners in the city.

Lunch, if you can call it that, at Il Panino del Chianti, Via de' Bardi 63r. A panino standing up, around six euros, three breads to choose from and all of them baked that morning. Glass of wine, two euros.

Uphill, 25 minutes

The Rampe, and the whole city

East along Via di San Niccolò, then up the Rampe del Poggi — a zigzag of ramps and fountains cut into the hillside, twenty-five minutes at a gentle climb. No stairs to speak of, and it delivers you straight onto the terrace.

Piazzale Michelangelo at 16:20, with the sun going down around 16:45. Duomo, Palazzo Vecchio, the bridges, the hills behind — the postcard, but earned on foot rather than by coach.

Five minutes higher

San Miniato al Monte

Almost nobody carries on up from the Piazzale, which is their loss. San Miniato is the finest Romanesque church in Tuscany — green and white marble on the front, a thousand-year-old floor inside, and a crypt older than most of the city below it.

In winter the monks sing Gregorian vespers in the late afternoon. Sit at the back. This is the best forty minutes of the day and it costs nothing.

Taxi rank at the Piazzale for the way down — five minutes back to the hotel, and time for a shower before dinner.

Nine o'clock

Steve Hackett

Dinner first at Flora & Fauno, downstairs in the hotel — fresh pasta, 19:30, no need to go anywhere for it.

Then the good part: the Teatro Cartiere Carrara is thirteen or fourteen minutes east along the Arno. Flat the whole way, same road, no bridge to cross. Walk it.

Out around half eleven. Walk back along the river if the night is kind, or take a taxi from the rank outside if it isn't.

Day two

Tuesday 17 November

Everything shut yesterday is open today. Two museums before lunch, two hours over a steak, half an hour buying presents, then trains all the way to Tel Aviv.

Booked, 09:30

The Accademia

Breakfast at eight, check out at 08:40, taxi to Santa Maria Novella — about fifteen minutes and fifteen euros. Bags into KiPoint left luggage: along platform 16, follow the blue Luggage Storage signs. Open 07:00–21:00, from six euros, a staffed counter rather than lockers.

Timed slot at 09:30, an hour inside. The David is the reason everyone comes, but the unfinished Prisoners in the corridor before him are the ones that stay with you — half-carved figures still stuck in the block.

Ten to two, closes

The Bargello

The quietest great museum in Florence, and the one people skip. Donatello's two Davids — the early marble and the extraordinary bronze — in a vaulted hall that used to be the town's prison and execution yard. Michelangelo's drunk, unsteady Bacchus downstairs.

It shuts around 13:50, so this is a morning job. An hour and a half is plenty.

The anchor of the trip

Regina Bistecca, 12:30

Via Ricasoli 14r, two minutes from the Accademia. Bistecca alla fiorentina in a room that used to be an antiquarian bookshop. Two hours, unhurried, table for one — nobody in Florence thinks that's strange.

Ask for la più piccola, the smallest they'll do. Somewhere between fifty and eighty euros, and it comes rare because that is the only way it is served. Don't ask for it otherwise.

Out at 14:30 sharp — there's shopping to do and the clock is tighter than it looks.

Thirty minutes, no more

Borgo San Lorenzo, and then go

Five minutes from the restaurant, and the last thing on the list. Half an hour is the whole budget — 14:40 to 15:10, and at 15:15 you walk, because the bags and the train don't move.

Hand luggage sets the rules: nothing wet, nothing that counts as a liquid, nothing that won't survive being wedged into a carry-on for two trains and a flight. That rules out oil, vinegar, honey and truffle paste, which is a gel whatever the label says. What's left is still the best of it.

Cantucci — the almond biscuits, boxed. The most obviously Florentine thing you can carry. Dried porcini — weighs nothing, costs little, better than anything sold at home. Saffron in small glass vials, over by the spices; Tuscany grows it. Pici, the fat hand-rolled local pasta nobody at home will have. Truffle salt or a dry porcini rub — the flavour, none of the liquid problem. Pecorino toscano, vacuum-sealed — fine in the cabin and it'll last the day easily, though it's the one thing needing the deli counter rather than a shelf. Chocolate or coffee to round out the numbers.

Ask for a sturdy bag at the till, or bring one. It has to survive forty-five minutes on your arm and then a carry-on. Cantucci boxes crush.

Home

Three legs to Tel Aviv

Frecciarossa FR9543 at 16:14, into Roma Termini at 17:49. Leonardo Express at 18:37, Fiumicino at 19:09. El Al LY384 at 21:50, landing 02:10.

Two and a half hours at the airport, which is exactly the right amount and not a minute more.
