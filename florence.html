Day one

Monday 16 November

Landing at lunchtime, and everything indoors is shut. So: the Oltrarno, the hill, and a concert.

Landing

In by one, out by half two

EN8194 from Munich touches down at 12:55. Hand luggage only, so it's straight through the terminal and into the taxi queue — Florence Airport is small enough that this is a genuinely quick business. Agree the fixed fare before getting in: around €25–28.

Ville sull'Arno sits on the Lungarno Cristoforo Colombo, a five-star villa on the river with a spa and a pool. Drop the bag, don't unpack, be back outside by 14:20.

The Uffizi, the Accademia, the Pitti and the Bargello are all closed on Mondays. That's not a problem — it's the reason the whole afternoon is outdoors and uphill. Nothing this afternoon has a closing door: if the taxi is slow, the sunset still happens.

The other bank

Ponte Vecchio, and east along the river

Taxi to the south end of the Ponte Vecchio. Lunch, if you can call it that, is right there at the start of the walk: Il Panino del Chianti, Via de' Bardi 63r. A panino standing up, around six euros, three breads to choose from and all of them baked that morning. Glass of wine, two euros.

Then east along the river — Lungarno Torrigiani into Lungarno Serristori. Open water, the city on the far bank, the Ponte Vecchio behind you. When you want shelter, duck inland to Via di San Niccolò: medieval, narrow, workshops with the doors open, and the oldest surviving street fabric in the Oltrarno.

Uphill, through a garden

The Rampe, the roses, the whole city

Porta San Niccolò at 15:45 — a 14th-century gate tower standing on its own in Piazza Poggi, the only one of Florence's gates that kept its full height.

Behind it, the Rampe del Poggi: terraced ramps with grottoes, fountain basins and stonework, restored and reopened in 2019 after decades shut. This is the garden — you're climbing through it.

Halfway up, directly on Viale Poggi, the Giardino delle Rose. Free, terraces and cypresses and benches, with the Duomo framed below. It closes at 17:00 in November, so don't dawdle down by the river.

Piazzale Michelangelo at 16:40, with the sun going down around 16:45. Duomo, Palazzo Vecchio, the bridges, the hills behind — the postcard, but earned on foot rather than by coach.

Five minutes higher

San Miniato al Monte

Almost nobody carries on up from the Piazzale, which is their loss. San Miniato is the finest Romanesque church in Tuscany — green and white marble on the front, a thousand-year-old floor inside, and a crypt older than most of the city below it.

In winter the monks sing Gregorian vespers in the late afternoon — if the timing lands, sit at the back. This is the best forty minutes of the day and it costs nothing.

Taxi rank at the Piazzale for the way down — five minutes back to the hotel, and time for a shower before dinner.

Nine o'clock

Steve Hackett

Dinner first at Flora & Fauno, downstairs in the hotel — fresh pasta, 19:30, no need to go anywhere for it. Tell them when you sit down that you're leaving at 20:35.

Then the good part: the Teatro Cartiere Carrara is thirteen or fourteen minutes east along the Arno. Flat the whole way, same road, no bridge to cross. Walk it.

Out around half eleven. Walk back along the river if the night is kind, or take a taxi from the rank outside if it isn't.

Day two

Tuesday 17 November

The David first thing, then the morning goes one of two ways depending on the sky. Two hours over a steak either way, then trains all the way to Tel Aviv.

Walk-in, 09:15

The Accademia

Breakfast at eight, check out at 08:40, taxi to Santa Maria Novella — about fifteen minutes and fifteen euros. Bags into KiPoint left luggage: along platform 16, follow the blue Luggage Storage signs. Open 07:00–21:00, from six euros, a staffed counter rather than lockers. It's poorly signposted, so allow ten minutes, and have the Frecciarossa booking ready to show at the platform gates.

Walk in at 09:15 — sixteen euros, and mid-November is off-peak, so there's no need to book. An hour inside. The David is the reason everyone comes, but the unfinished Prisoners in the corridor before him are the ones that stay with you — half-carved figures still stuck in the block.

Out at 10:15, and here the day forks. Decide on Monday night: clear sky, go up to Fiesole; rain, stay under roofs. Nothing needs rebooking except the restaurant time.

Version A

If it's clear: Fiesole

The Etruscan hill town five kilometres above Florence — older than Florence itself.

Bus 7, 25 minutes up

Up the hill

Out of the Accademia and up Via Ricasoli — two minutes to Piazza San Marco and Bus 7, which runs every twenty minutes. Buy the ticket first at a tabaccheria (the black-and-white "T"); a biglietto multiplo covers both ways. Validate it on board.

From Piazza Mino, ten to fifteen minutes uphill to the Monastery of San Francesco. It's free, and the panorama over the Arno valley is the whole point. Then down through the village, past the Romanesque Duomo, for a coffee in the piazza before the 12:05 bus back.

The archaeological area with the Roman theatre is probably closed on Tuesdays from November to February — sources conflict. Go for the hill, the air and the view; treat the ruins as a bonus.

Version B

If it rains: markets and interiors

Everything under a roof, and never more than about twelve minutes between cover.

Iron and glass, 1874

Mercato Centrale

Seven minutes' walk from the Accademia. The ground floor is the working market — Monday to Saturday, 07:00 to 14:00 — and it's best before eleven, fully stocked and less crowded. Do the gift shopping here. Cash is preferred at the ground-floor stalls.

Then upstairs to the food hall, open daily from ten until midnight, two dozen counters and more. A coffee, a glass of wine, and then an eight-minute walk to Via Ricasoli for 12:30.

Founded 1221

The Officina, Santa Maria Novella

Via della Scala 16. The Dominican apothecary, founded in 1221 and open to the public since 1612 — the oldest pharmacy in Europe. Free to walk in, open daily 09:30–20:00. Frescoed vaults, antique glass, and a smell you'll remember.

It also solves the gifts: soaps, rose water, potpourri — all dry, all carry-on safe. Five minutes from here to the station.

The anchor of the trip

Regina Bistecca

Via Ricasoli 14r, two minutes from the Accademia. Lunch service from 12:30 — keep that time if it's raining, move it to 12:45 if it's Fiesole. Two hours, unhurried, table for one — nobody in Florence thinks that's strange.

Priced per hundred grams: the house selection €6.90, Angus €8.50, Chianina €9.80. Chianina is the ancient Tuscan breed and the authentic choice — about €78 for an 800g cut. All of it dry-aged 21 days or more and grilled over charcoal.

Ask for la più piccola. One solo diner reports being given the smallest cut in the house, just under a kilo, no fuss. It comes rare, because that is the tradition. Don't ask for it cooked through.

This is the anchor of the trip, not a refuelling stop. Allow the full two hours.

Dry goods only

Gifts that fit a carry-on

Hand luggage sets the rules: no liquids over 100ml. Olive oil, vin santo, balsamic, wine and truffle paste are all out — unless a bag gets checked at Fiumicino. What's left is still the best of it.

Cantucci — the almond biscuits, boxed. Dried porcini — weighs nothing, better than anything sold at home. Saffron; Tuscany grows it. Pici or other pasta shapes. Truffle salt — the flavour, none of the liquid problem. Pecorino, vacuum-packed. Coffee and chocolate to round out the numbers. And on the rainy version, soaps and rose water from the Officina.

Clear day: Sapori & Dintorni, Borgo San Lorenzo 15/17, straight after lunch. Rainy day: the Mercato Centrale's ground floor, before lunch.

Home

Three legs to Tel Aviv

Frecciarossa FR9543 at 16:14, into Roma Termini at 17:49. The Leonardo Express leaves from platforms 23–24, right at the far eastern end of the station — allow ten minutes to walk there. Every fifteen minutes, flat fare, no assigned seats; buy at a machine or the counter on the day and validate before boarding, unless you're using the app.

At Terminal 3 by 19:20, two and a half hours before El Al LY384 at 21:50. El Al runs its own security questioning ahead of the normal airport checks, which is why the margin is that size. Landing 02:10.
